As the aging population grows and home health aides drop out, caregivers face several key pain points:

1. **Workload Increase**: With fewer aides, existing caregivers must handle more patients, leading to increased stress and burnout.
2. **Staff Shortages**: Recruiting and retaining home health aides becomes challenging, disrupting continuity of care.
3. **Financial Strain**: Higher turnover rates and recruitment costs can strain healthcare budgets.
4. **Quality of Care Concerns**: Reduced staff can compromise the quality of care provided, potentially leading to adverse health outcomes.
5. **Emotional and Physical Exhaustion**: Caregivers may experience emotional and physical exhaustion due to the increased workload and lack of support.
6. **Limited Resources**: Insufficient training, equipment, and support resources can exacerbate the challenges faced by caregivers.
7. **Client Satisfaction Issues**: Decreased staff availability can lead to delays or gaps in service, affecting client satisfaction and trust.
8. **Regulatory Compliance Challenges**: Maintaining compliance with healthcare regulations becomes more difficult with reduced staff, potentially leading to legal issues.
9. **Mental Health Impact**: Caregivers may experience higher levels of anxiety, depression, and other mental health issues due to the increased pressure.
10. **Career Burnout**: The unsustainable workload can lead to career burnout, causing experienced caregivers to leave the profession altogether.
